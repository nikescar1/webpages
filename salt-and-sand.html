What we do

Traction where you actually walk and drive.

Anchorage doesn’t get one kind of winter. It gets glaze ice in December, a thaw in January, and packed snow in March. Each one needs a different material and a different timing — that’s the whole job.

Driveway salting

Calibrated spreader passes that break the bond between ice and pavement, so your tires find grip on the slope out to the street.

  • Rock salt, treated salt or blended melt
  • Apron and street-edge windrow included
  • Effective well below 15°F with treated blend

Walkway & step sanding

Hand-spread traction grit on stairs, landings, sidewalks and mailbox paths — the places where a fall actually happens.

  • Works when it’s too cold for salt to melt
  • Safe on pavers, brick and stamped concrete
  • Spring sweep-up available

Questions

The things people ask before signing up.

Do you plow snow too?
De-icing is our specialty, so we don’t run plow blades ourselves. Most of our customers pair us with a plow service, and we’re happy to coordinate timing so the salt goes down on a cleared surface instead of getting scraped off an hour later. Tell us who plows for you and we’ll sequence around them.
Salt or sand — which do I actually need?
Salt melts ice, but its effectiveness falls off sharply below about 15°F, which Anchorage clears regularly in midwinter. Sand doesn’t melt anything — it gives you traction immediately at any temperature. Most properties do best with salt on the driving surface and sand on steps and walks, and we shift the mix as temperatures move. You never have to make that call yourself.
Will this damage my concrete, my dog’s paws, or my lawn?
Concrete under one year old should not see chloride de-icers at all — tell us and we’ll use traction sand or a low-chloride blend on those surfaces. We also offer a pet-paw-safe product for walkways and entries. Beyond material choice, the biggest factor is spread rate: we calibrate to the minimum that works, which keeps tracking, runoff and edge burn down.
How fast do you get there after it snows?
Seasonal customers are typically treated within about four hours of snowfall ending, and we aim to complete residential routes before 7 a.m. so your morning is normal. During a genuinely major event — the kind that closes the Glenn — routes run longer, and we’ll text you a realistic window rather than a wishful one. On Call customers are usually served within 24 hours of the request.
Am I locked into a contract?
No. Season Pass is billed monthly and you can pause or cancel with a week’s notice — you’re only charged for months you’ve started. There’s no auto-renewal into next winter unless you tell us to put you back on the route.
What if I’m out of town all winter?
That’s one of the most common reasons people hire us. Snowbirds and travelers get the same photo confirmations, so you can see from anywhere that the walk to your door is passable — which also matters for keeping the place looking occupied and for anyone checking on it.
Do you handle commercial liability documentation?
Yes. Every commercial visit is logged with arrival time, material used, quantity and photos of treated surfaces. We provide a certificate of insurance on request and can export a full season’s service record if you ever need to document conditions on a specific date.
